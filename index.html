The short version

03

I started in growth marketing, which in practice meant being handed a budget and a target and being made responsible for the distance between them. Over nine years and more than $800,000 in ad spend, I learned that the campaigns were rarely the constraint. What broke was everything underneath: the CRM nobody trusted, the handoff that lost half the leads, the reporting that could not say which channel produced revenue.

That is the problem I ended up building a company around. HubXpert exists because most businesses do not need more traffic. They need the machinery between marketing and revenue to actually work.

Two years later I did something unrelated on purpose. Time Traveler is a physical retail brand: inventory, purchasing, merchandising, walk in customers, cash flow you can feel. There is no attribution model in retail. Someone either walks out with the bag or they do not.

I run both. One serves clients across three continents. The other is a shop where I know the regulars by name. The lessons cross over more than you would expect.

Where I actually add value

04

Four areas, built over nine years. Most companies I talk to have a problem in one of them and a symptom in another.

01

RevOps and CRM architecture

Where the other three either compound or leak. Lifecycle and pipeline design, lead scoring and routing, marketing to sales handoff, data hygiene, CRM integrations, and reporting a leadership team will actually trust. Across HubXpert we have built more than 3,000 automations, and this is the layer where most implementations quietly fail.

02

Outbound that gets replies

Cold email infrastructure, list building, sequencing, deliverability and automated LinkedIn outreach. For US and UK clients we consistently hit around 70% open rates — less from clever copy than from domain warmup, list hygiene and sending discipline most teams skip.

03

Organic search, content and AI visibility

SEO as a compounding asset rather than a monthly report: topic architecture, technical foundations, programmatic pages at scale, internal linking. More recently answer engine optimisation — content structured to be retrieved and cited by AI assistants, quietly a meaningful share of how B2B buyers now find vendors.

04

Demand generation and paid acquisition

More than $800,000 managed across Meta, Google and LinkedIn, including a 150 lakh BDT Facebook budget executed personally as a Facebook certified media buyer. The work I care about is the part after the campaign build: UTM discipline, source level attribution, and knowing week to week which channel produces qualified pipeline rather than qualified looking leads.

If your marketing works but your revenue does not reflect it, the problem is almost always in the first column.

Alongside running HubXpert, I work directly with a small number of B2B companies on the problem underneath most growth plateaus. Not the campaigns, but the system the campaigns run on.

Most companies I speak to are not short of activity. They are running ads, publishing content, sending outbound. What they cannot say with confidence is which of it produces revenue, where qualified buyers are lost between marketing and sales, or what the next hire should be spent on. That is the work.

It begins with a structured look at how demand moves through your business: where it comes from, what happens inside the CRM, where it stalls, and what the numbers say once measured properly. What follows depends on what the review turns up. Some want a plan they can run themselves. Others want me involved for longer.

Strategy engagements often turn into build work. When they do, my team at HubXpert usually does it, and I will tell you that up front. When another partner is a better fit, I will tell you that too.

Building AI agents for revenue teams

The question is not whether AI belongs in the marketing stack, but what happens when agents move into the operating layer. At HubXpert we are building agents that work inside the CRM: qualifying and routing, keeping data clean, drafting follow up, surfacing deals that have gone quiet.

Taking a Platinum practice toward Elite

Elite is the tier above Platinum and there are very few of them. Getting there is not a marketing exercise. It is a function of retained clients, delivery quality and depth of technical work, and it shapes most of the operating decisions I make.

Building a company that does not need me

A business dependent on its founder is not a business, it is a job with more risk. I spend an increasing share of my time on the things that make that untrue: hiring, delegating properly, documenting how decisions get made, and resisting the pull to involve myself in every one.

Writing about what I am learning

Mostly on LinkedIn, occasionally at longer length. What actually works in B2B growth, where HubSpot implementations go wrong, and what running a retail business has taught me about running a technology company.
